# CapstoneNetworkDelta
Delta group CYB495 ISP Network Proposition, connecting Washington, DC metro area (National Institutes of Health). Los Angeles metro area (Cedars-Sinai). Rochester, Minnesota (Mayo Clinic). 


Request for Proposal – 1020-2017
Information Technology Infrastructure Proposal
I. Statement of Purpose
a. Current Internet Service Providers (ISP) are not focused on the needs of health
care professional organizations and their regulation and security needs.
b. This proposal will create a new ISP to meet the challenges of the health care
industry and ensure the security of our patients, medical offices, and hospitals
where security concerns are the highest in the world.
c. The new ISP will deliver state of the art internet services to our customers while
meeting all federal and state requirements for ensuring the security of patient
information and electronic access to medical records.
d. By using the same medial ISP all member offices will have access to the correct
level of patient information to ensure safety and security for their patients.
e. The new ISP will become the repository of patient information that can be
accesses by medical professionals, hospitals, pharmacies, and patients
themselves through a cloud based infrastructure thereby ensuring the best care
for our patients available by medical providers.
II. Background Information
a. The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act, or HIPAA, is a set of
regulations that became law in 1996.
b. HIPPA’s purpose is to help people carry their health insurance from one
company to the next, as well as streamline the movement of medical records
from one health care institution to another.
c. In addition, HIPAA created a system to recognize and enforce the rights of
patients to protect the privacy of their medical records.
d. In 2003 the HIPPA privacy act was implemented giving individual’s specific rights
regarding protected health information and obligations of healthcare providers,
health plans, and health care clearinghouses.
e. In general healthcare providers, health plans and clearinghouses are prohibited
from disclosing health information except as authorized by the patient or
specifically permitted by regulation.
III. Scope of work
a. The new ISP, which will be created by this proposal, will be responsible for
internet connections for all member health organizations as well as collecting all
electronic medical information for patients in those organizations and placing
them in a secure cloud based network which is accessible to all member health
organizations.
b. The new ISP will be responsible for meeting all federal and state regulations
required for the use of electronic health information.
IV. Outcome and Performance standards
a. By creating this ISP all member health organizations will be free of the
requirements of federal and state regulations that will become the responsibility
of the ISP.
b. All steps will be taken by the ISP to set-up and ensure the health provider’s
network and security.
c. All medical patient records will be kept “in the cloud” in the ISPs servers and
network.
d. All locations will be given redundant network access to ensure service regardless
of network outages and failures.
e. A network operations center (NOC) will be established and manned 24/7 to act
on any network outages or failures that occur.
V. Deliverables
a. The physical network has been in development for more than 6 months and is
very close to going online.
b. Unfortunately, the “back-end” network plan has failed and will not be available
when the network goes live in 5 weeks.
c. The requirement for this request for proposal is to create and implement the
back-end network which will be designed to network all systems together and
collect and distribute patient records to all health care organizations meeting all
federal and state requirements for medical record access.
VI. Scope of work
a. The current network will be very small only covering 3 large health organizations
in the country.
b. The three areas being covered in the initial roll-out are:
i. Washington, DC metro area (National Institutes of Health)
ii. Los Angeles metro area (Cedars-Sinai)
iii. Rochester, Minnesota (Mayo Clinic)
c. There will initially be only a single medical group connecting to the system in
each area, but the system must be robust enough to quickly grow to hundreds of
health care professional organizations in each area.
d. After initial successful roll-out in the target cities additional networks will be
created in:
i. Baltimore, MD (Johns Hopkins)
ii. Boston, Massachusetts (Massachusetts General)
iii. Houston, TX (Texas Medical Center)
VII. Term of contract
a. The term of contract will be for one year at which time an audit will be
conducted to ensure proper conduct of the contract and security level provided
by the ISP.
b. If the contract is successful, a contract renewal of up to five years will be
instituted with annual reviews to ensure continued adherence to all federal and
state regulations and requirements.
VIII. Request for Proposal requirements
a. The first week will be a formal document detailing the requirements of the RFP
and the group’s proposal of how operations will proceed. The document must
contain the network requirements as well as the storage and security measures
that will be taken to ensure meeting all federal and state requirements for
electronic health records.
b. Week 2 will be a “mock-up” of the network using Cisco Packet Tracer to show all
network and server requirements for the network. The mock-up must be a
working model showing how information and documents will move around the
network and security measures that are in place to ensure security
requirements. The network mock-up will also show how the health care
providers will access the Internet and ensure standardized network access to all
systems in their network. Since all records are retained by the ISP the main
concern is to allow access to those authorized to do so while allowing others
unfettered access to the Internet.
c. Week 3 will be a presentation (30 minutes) of the new network and how it will
meet the requirements of the RFP. After this presentation the RFP will be
awarded and work will proceed to the physical network and how the records will
be stored and protected from disclosure outside of authorized groups.
d. Week 4 will be a physical implementation to the three groups in the initial roll-
out of the ISP. National Institutes of Health, Cedars-Sinai, and the Mayo Clinic
will convert to the new network and all Internet traffic will begin using the new
system.
e. Week 5, a final presentation showing how the new network is working, what
plans are in place for its rapid expansion, and how the new network will meet
federal and state security and portability requirements.
